1. Binomial & Poisson Multi-Box Hit Aggregation

Each box format carries an independent expected yield of serial-numbered parallels based on pack ratios:

  • 2026 Topps Chrome Marvel Value Box: ~0.48 serial-numbered cards per box (~1 in 2.1 boxes)
  • 2026 Topps Chrome Neon Mega Box: ~1.05 serial-numbered cards per box
  • 2026 Topps Marvel Neon Hobby Box: ~2.00 serial-numbered cards per box
E(Total_Numbered) = (N_val × 0.48) + (N_mega × 1.05) + (N_hobby × 2.00)
P(At Least 1 Target Hit) = 1 - (1 - p_single_hit)^(E_Total_Pool)

2. Post-Release Market Depletion & Loose-Box Bias Model

Standard hobby calculators assume a Day 1 untouched print run where 100% of cards and boxes remain sealed. However, months after release, buying loose boxes on the secondary market introduces two mathematical shifts:

A. Exponential Supply Decay

Trading card breaking velocity is heavily front-loaded during the first 60 days of release. We model the percentage of global supply opened after m months using an exponential decay constant (λ = 0.35):

Depletion(m) = 1 - e^(-0.35 × m)

B. Proportional Depletion vs. "Dead-Case" Selection Bias

Under random sampling, high-numbered parallels (/75, /99, /150, /199) deplete at the exact same rate as boxes, keeping per-box odds stable. However, ultra-rare grails (1/1 Superfractors, /5 Reds, /10s) suffer from Adverse Selection on the loose secondary market when sellers stop opening a case after hitting the case chase:

Adjusted_Prob(tier, m) = Day1_Prob × [ 1 - (α_tier × Depletion(m)) ]
  • 1/1 Superfractors (α = 0.45): Up to a 45% penalty in loose secondary-market boxes at full maturity.
  • /5 to /10 Parallels (α = 0.20 - 0.25): Moderate loose-box survival reduction.
  • /50 to /199 Parallels (α = 0.00): Unaffected by case-stopping bias; odds match Day 1 pack ratios.
